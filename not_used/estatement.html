From a 3D Cube to a Website

The 3D Cube represented the youthful and adaptable nature of play-doh, which reflected how I am a student engineer that is preparing to face the real world. That identity still hasn't gone away in this website format: the typeface is built off of my handwriting, the purple hues symbolize engineering, and the content can be easily edited like a mesh.

However, when I revisited the Cube, I realized I wanted to be able to represent more than 6 ideas on 6 faces. Perhaps I could add a new face, but at one point, the geometry would be too complicated to depict an idea clearly. Instead, I decided to bring all my ideas onto a website, so that I could always add more pages to build my position statement.

Adding onto the Original Definition

I added onto my definition of engineering design not only because I've taken part in more projects, like ESC102 showcase, but also because I spent time reanalyzing past projects that occured before January. Now that I've been to most Praxis lectures and studio (I watched the recordings when I couldn't wake up to 9 AMs), I've acquired and developed stronger CTMFs and reflection skills.

Now, I believe that engineering design is not just using iterative processes to create the best design. It also requires preparation for risks and obstacles that present themselves when bringing the design to reality, and using verification and validation to convince the stakeholders to accept the recommended design.

To better understand why I updated my perspective, I've formatted this website as a journey through three major projects that resculpted my definition of engineering design: ESC101, CIV102, and ESC102.
